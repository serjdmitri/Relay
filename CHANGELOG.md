# Changelog

## 0.38.13

- **Fixes from your report on false strangers, standby locking, and a dead mouse after an alarm.** (1) A face the landmark finder does not confirm (a poster, a reflection, a guitar) is no longer judged as a stranger, and a black picture (lid closed with Vorssaint keeping the Mac awake, or a covered camera) is skipped instead of counting as "you left" or "nobody". (2) With the standby screen on, leaving no longer also locks the Mac (the lock fired after a minute of you sitting still or looking away), and while standby is showing Relay holds the display awake; Vorssaint or any keep-awake tool is unaffected, the two add up. (3) A guard screen nothing wants any more now comes down within seconds (a check every second), unlocking the Mac with your password or fingerprint clears an alarm and any guard screen, and a new input release hands the mouse back (a full-screen overlay window can no longer be left catching clicks) after every alarm, lock and unlock. 5 new tests.
- **"What do you see" now opens the camera view, and offline mode picks a model that fits a 24 GB Mac.** Asking what Jarvis sees opens the floating camera view first (so you see what is being looked at, with the boxes), then describes it: with a model that can see pictures it gets the picture and the names the object finder recognised; with one that cannot (a local text model) it gets just the names, so "what do you see" works offline too. For offline thinking, the old default (qwen2.5:14b, about 9 GB) is too big next to Firefox, Claude and the rest on 24 GB, so going offline now uses qwen2.5:7b (4.7 GB) unless you already have the 14B installed; a vision model (qwen2.5vl:7b, 6 GB) is the one to add for describing pictures, and Ollama should hold one model at a time.
- **The camera view now floats with faded edges, named boxes, and voice control.** "Show my camera" opens the live picture (mirrored) with its edges melting into the screen, and a box with a low-opacity fill and a name ("person 91%", "cup 74%", 80 everyday kinds of object) round each thing it sees, found live on this Mac by a small bundled model (Google's EfficientDet-Lite0, 7.3 MB, Apache 2.0; nothing is saved or sent). Say: "make the camera bigger / smaller", "move the camera left / right / up / down", "make the camera full screen", "camera normal size", "fade the edges more / less", "hide the boxes" / "show the boxes", "only show people on camera", "highlight the cups and phones in the camera", "show everything". "Highlight the buttons on my screen" uses the screen-drawing tool and now labels each box with the element's name. Ollama is installed on this Mac for offline mode; it is not started and has no model yet. 2 new tests.
- **"Go offline" / "go online".** One sentence switches Jarvis's thinking between the cloud model and a local model on this Mac (Ollama), by voice or text ("go offline", "use the local model", "go online", "which model are you using"). It remembers which cloud model you had and restores it, checks that Ollama is running and has the model, and tells you plainly what is missing and what you lose: a local model is weaker at using tools and long requests, and to describe the camera it needs a vision model such as qwen2.5vl:7b. Nothing is downloaded for you: it says the one command to run (qwen2.5:7b is about 4.7 GB). Speech-to-text (Apple's on-device dictation) and the voice (OmniVoice) were already on this Mac, so offline here means only the thinking moves. Your phone's Jarvis still needs this Mac to be on. 4 new tests.
- **"Show my camera" and "what do you see".** Say "show my camera" (or "show me my face") and a small floating window shows the live camera, mirrored; say "hide the camera" (or press Esc) to close it. Nothing is saved or sent. Ask "what do you see", "look at me" or "what am I holding up" and Jarvis takes one picture and describes it, using a model that can see pictures (Claude can; a local model needs a vision model). It uses the camera Relay already has open for owner recognition, and otherwise opens the camera window so you can see that the camera is being used: Relay does not look through the camera without showing you. 4 new tests.
- **The orb option is gone for now.** The Reactor / Orb choice is removed from Settings and Relay always draws the reactor. (The orb drawing code is still there, switched off, so it can come back.)
- **The guard catches strangers fast and stops alarming on you.** Real data from your Mac: an alarm started on you after 13 looks at a score of 0.76 to 0.83 (a strong match) because you had not blinked while staring at the screen. A matching face with no blink now counts against you only after a full minute of it (a photo is still caught, slowly); a clearly different face is still caught within a few looks. And a face that is turned about 30 degrees or looks down about 20 degrees (at your phone, the keyboard or the room) is now treated as not judgeable, where it used to need 40 and 25, so you not looking at the computer no longer reads as a stranger. A stranger who is using the Mac faces the screen and is judged as before.
- **Jarvis Navigation without your Mac (standalone page).** Open https://serjdmitri.github.io/Relay/nav/ on the phone and add it to the Home Screen. `npm run build:standalone` packages the phone navigation page as plain files in `docs/nav/`, which GitHub Pages serves. It asks Photon, Valhalla and OpenFreeMap directly from the phone (the same replies and checks as the Mac's, compared by a test), so it needs neither the Mac nor Tailscale; being on https, the microphone and location work by themselves. It can be added to the Home Screen. Everything on the phone page works there: turn-by-turn voice, stops, home and work (saved on the phone), the centre button, "Hey Jarvis". It waits a moment between requests to be kind to the free services. The phone's internet address is visible to those services (before, only the Mac's was). Tried live against the real services from a sub-folder: search, route, directions and tiles, and no call to a Mac. The page served by the Mac is unchanged in behaviour. It is a public page with no secrets in it. 8 new tests.
- **The camera uses less of the Mac when nobody is in front of it.** Measured on your Mac: Relay's helper processes were using about 40% of a core, the largest part being the face landmark finder, which ran about 8 times a second all the time. It now runs about twice a second when nobody is in view and at full speed (so blinks are still caught) as soon as someone is. Recognition and the stranger alarm are unchanged. Tailscale itself was using about 0% CPU.
- **Jarvis missing at unlock, and drawn wrongly afterwards.** The reactor's windows sit hidden between uses, and after a screen lock or sleep they can come back blank or glitchy, so at unlock only the recap card appeared and the next Option press drew Jarvis badly. Relay now replaces its idle reactor windows with fresh ones the moment you unlock or the Mac wakes (and loads them ahead of use); a window that is showing something is left alone. I could not reproduce the lock itself (a fresh window draws the greeting reactor and the recap card side by side correctly), so this fixes the most likely cause; if it still happens, send a screenshot of how Jarvis looks when it is broken.
- **Shuffle and repeat buttons on the phone now turn off again.** Pressing shuffle or repeat twice said "on" both times. Spotify can keep reporting the old state after a change, and a toggle that trusted that report always chose "on". Relay now reads the setting back after changing it; if the app does not show what was just set, it stops trusting the app's answer and toggles from what it last set. In Apple Music, repeat now flips off and all ("repeat one" by name) instead of cycling through three states. (I could not see Spotify's real behaviour while it was paused, so this is built to work whichever way it reports; tell me if it still sticks.)
- **The map follows you from the first second, stops work, and the controls match.** Start now puts the camera on you, tilted and turned the way you are heading, instead of waiting for the next position (a phone screenshot showed the whole route still zoomed out right after Start). While you navigate, the map fills the screen and the controls sit in a compact strip at the bottom (no dead space under them). A centre button (◎) is always on the map while you navigate: lit while the map follows you, plain after you move the map; tap it to snap back to you (or say "recenter"). The controls are four matching buttons (Talk, Voice, Map, End), and the Hey Jarvis button says "Hey". Stops: say "take me to Starbucks and then home", "take me home with a stop at Starbucks", or "add a stop at the bank" during the trip. At each stop Jarvis says "You have arrived at Starbucks, Sir", then "Say continue when you are ready for home"; "continue" (or the button) routes from where you are to the next stop. Home and work are saved on the phone only: stand there and say "this is home" (or "save my location as work"), then "take me home". Until you save it, Jarvis says it does not know where home is. Up to four places in one trip. Tested in the real page with a simulated trip of three stops and 1 new test.
- **A friend who kept turning or leaning at the Mac no longer slips past the alarm.** The alarm needs five clear looks at a stranger in a row, and any look that could not be judged (face too small, turned away, in the grey zone) reset that count to zero. A real case from your Mac: 24 seconds with 9 stranger looks, 12 unjudgeable and 1 unsure, interleaved, so the count never reached five and nothing happened. Looks that cannot be judged now pause the count instead of erasing it. Your own face, an empty chair, or more than 12 unclear looks in a row still reset it, so you looking away does not start it. "Why didn't it alert" gave the right reason ("too small, too far or turned away"); this fixes the cause.
- **"Hey Jarvis" on the phone.** The phone page now has a switch in its menu (and an ear button on the navigation map) that listens for "Hey Jarvis" with the phone's own speech recognition. Say "Hey Jarvis, lock my Mac" and it is sent like a typed command, or say "Hey Jarvis" alone and then the command; on the map, "Hey Jarvis, what's next" works while you drive. It accepts how the recogniser tends to mishear it ("hey Jarvus", "ok Harvis", a bare "Jarvis" at the start). It pauses while the phone is speaking or you hold the reactor, so it does not hear itself, and it stops with a clear message if the microphone is refused or the speech service keeps failing. Honest limits: a web page can only listen while it is open on screen with the phone unlocked (the page keeps the screen awake while it is on, which uses battery), it is off until you turn it on, and Safari wants one tap after the page opens before the microphone starts. Not like the Mac, which listens all the time. Tested with 7 new tests and a probe of the real pages with a fake recogniser (it also found and fixed a browser-only bug in the timer handling).
- **"Hey Jarvis" and the Option key can no longer both go dead together.** While you record a new shortcut, Relay pauses its shortcuts and "Hey Jarvis" so the key you press is not also obeyed. That pause was only ever turned off by the settings window, so closing, hiding or reloading it mid-recording left both paused until Relay restarted, with the screen still saying Ready. The pause now ends by itself after 15 seconds and whenever the window is hidden, loses focus or reloads. (Found by reading the code after both stopped at once; I could not confirm on your Mac that this is what happened, since Relay writes no log.)
- **Jarvis Navigation on the phone: turn-by-turn guidance, like Apple Maps, with the map floating at the top.** The "take me to…" page is now a real navigation screen. The map sits at the top with its edges fading into the dark (the God's Eye look) and Jarvis's colours; the trip is in a panel below it (arrival time, time left, distance to go). Say or type "take me to Starbucks", "walk to the beach", then "start": the page follows your position, tilts and turns the map with you, shows the next turn at the top, and Jarvis speaks it as you approach ("In half a mile, turn left onto Beta Street", then "In 800 feet…", then "Turn left."; "You have arrived at Starbucks, Sir."). Wrong turn: "Recalculating, Sir." and a new route. The microphone button takes spoken commands (start, stop, mute, repeat, what's next, how far, zoom, overview, recenter, Apple Maps). The voice is the phone's own British male voice (Daniel on an iPhone), not the Mac's. Honest limits: a web page on an iPhone stops guiding when it is in the background or the phone locks (the screen is kept awake while navigating, and Apple Maps is one tap away), live position needs the secure connection the phone already uses, and there is no always-on "Hey Jarvis" on the phone: tap the microphone. Tested with a simulated drive through the real page (spoken lines, banner, wrong-turn reroute, arrival) and 15 new tests.
- **"Enroll my face" no longer leaves the reactor in front of the setup window.** Its reply is now short and tells Relay not to hold it on screen (and not to ask "anything else?"), so the reactor goes after a moment, and the setup window sits above the reactor and the answer card. Only this command changed; every other answer stays until dismissed as before.
- **Trusted people are now recognised as themselves.** Before, a trusted person's face was mixed into the list that means "you", so Relay took them for you, and the second-viewer guard counted them as a stranger and started its lock countdown. Now the camera page compares each face with you and, separately, with your trusted profiles. A trusted person (who has also blinked, so a photo of them does not count) is neither you nor a stranger: they never start the access-denied alarm, they are not counted as an extra viewer, they cannot arm the guard or lift the standby screen as if they were you, and an alarm that a stranger started ends once only trusted people are left in view. A stranger beside a trusted person still starts the alarm and the countdown. Tested with a real camera (your face as "trusted": trusted score 0.87, owner score -0.09, flagged trusted in 31 of 31 looks; as "owner": owner 0.85, trusted -0.11, flagged trusted 0 of 23).
- **"Use Twilio for calls" / "switch to the free calling model".** A voice command that sets only which provider places calls (Twilio, Bland, or automatic). It never takes or changes a key or token: for Twilio it tells you that the account SID, auth token and Twilio phone number are yours to add in Calls settings, and that automatic check-ins stay paused until recreated, because they were authorized for the old provider. The reply after "call me about..." now names the provider actually used instead of always saying Bland. Twilio reads the message in the British male voice Brian (Polly.Brian); a scheduled, authorized call through Twilio is covered by a new test.
- **"Take me to…" on the phone now shows a route preview inside Relay (finished from Codex's work).** On the phone page, "take me to NSU University School", "walk me to Starbucks" or "directions to Miami Airport" opens a Jarvis Navigation page with an interactive map (MapLibre, OpenStreetMap data from OpenFreeMap), place search (Photon) and a walking or driving route with distance, time and step-by-step directions (Valhalla). It replaces the "Preview in Jarvis Navigation" link that needed a separate iPhone app that was never installed; "Open Apple Maps for live navigation" stays as the way to navigate for real. It started as a preview only (live turn-by-turn guidance is the next item). Location is optional and read once, only when you tap its button (or search for a starting place instead). These are free public services meant for light use and can be unavailable, in which case the page says so and offers Apple Maps. The requests carry no pairing token and no AI model sees them, they are blocked in local/private mode, and the page works only for a paired phone. Settings > Phone shows a "Phone map providers" card to switch to your own compatible servers (HTTPS or localhost only, no credentials in the URLs). MapLibre adds about 29 MB to the Mac app; the phone loads only its browser files. Found and fixed while testing against the live server: Valhalla returns its route line in an encoded form even when asked for plain coordinates, so Relay now decodes it (bounded, with malformed shapes rejected). Tested: place search, routes, caching, rate limits, private mode and the paired-phone checks, a live route request, and the page itself at a phone-sized window (map, route, directions, and a cancelled route not coming back late).
- **Relay learns the people you name and starts naming them itself.** When you name a visitor (in the pictures window, or by saying "that's Sam"), Relay keeps their face as a profile (encrypted, up to 12 faces, never a picture). After you have named someone a couple of times, a new visitor who looks like them gets a question instead of an assumption: "Is that Sam? Say yes or no", asked when you come back, spoken if replies are spoken, and with Yes / No buttons in the pictures window. Each yes adds the face; a no records that face as not them (it is never offered as them again) and sets the learning back; "no, that's Alex" names the visitor in the same breath. After three yeses in a row the person is learned and later visits are named without asking ("Sam (I recognised the face from earlier visits)"). A face must match well and clearly better than anyone else you have named to be offered; a face that looks like nobody is never named. A plain "yes" or "no" is only heard for five minutes after Relay asked, so it is never taken for anything else. "Who do you know?" lists the people; "forget Sam's face" (or the Forget button, after Touch ID) removes a profile and its name from their visits. Before, a visit whose face matched a named one simply copied the name; now a name only comes from you or from a person Relay has learned and you have confirmed. Checked with the faces from a real scan: after two namings it asked for the next three angles (scores 0.73 to 0.86), learned the person after three yeses, named the rest by itself, and never suggested an unrelated face.
- **Pictures of strangers, and "who was at your Mac".** When the access-denied alarm starts for someone who is not you (never the test screen), Relay takes up to three pictures of the camera view, a few seconds apart, and keeps them encrypted on this Mac (the same protection as your face data, as separate files; the newest 30 visits for up to 60 days). It also keeps the stranger's face numbers, so a second visit by the same face is recognised ("someone I have seen before", with the date of the earlier visit). When you come back (the first time the camera sees you after the alarm, or when you unlock the Mac) Relay tells you: who was there as far as it can know (it cannot know a name, so it says "someone I do not recognise", or "someone I have seen before"), when, how many pictures and how it ended (you came back, your fingerprint, a trusted person, or the screen locked), and opens a window with the pictures. In that window you can give a visit a name, and later visits by the same face carry it; deleting a visit asks you to confirm with Touch ID first, so a stranger cannot erase the evidence. Say "show the intruder pictures" or "who was at my computer" to see them any time, "turn off intruder photos" to stop taking them, and "delete the intruder photos" to remove them all (that also asks for confirmation). Tested with a real camera (snapshots, stored encrypted, the same face recognised on a second visit).
- **After saving your face, Relay shows how accurate and reliable the scan was, and offers to redo weak angles.** Once the nine poses are saved it asks you to look at the camera for a moment (a live check with fresh looks that were not part of the scan), then shows three numbers and a chip for each angle. Accuracy is the share of scanned angles that are recognised from the other angles; reliability is how far above the match threshold they score on average (0% at the threshold, 100% at 0.9, set from a real scan of the owner); the live check is how many of the fresh looks were recognised. A scan is "reliable" at 90% accuracy and 80% reliability with no weak angle. Below that you are told which angles were weakest and offered "Redo the weak angles" (only those are captured again, and the straight-ahead position is kept) or "Redo everything"; below 75% it says the scan is not reliable. It is an estimate from one sitting, so a different room or light can score lower.
- **The build's startup check no longer calls a build broken when the Mac is locked.** Relay reads the Keychain when it starts, which cannot be done while the screen is locked, so a check run then hung and looked like a failure. It now notices the locked screen, waits up to 15 minutes for it to be unlocked, and if it stays locked says so and stops (instead of reporting a broken build). It also retries once on a plain failure, and no longer mistakes a leftover macOS SecurityAgent process for an open Keychain prompt.
- **A fingerprint stop means it was you.** When the alarm is stopped with your fingerprint, the pictures taken for it are deleted and never shown: that was you, not an intruder.
- **It keeps guarding while you are away, even after Relay restarts.** The guard only begins to guard once it has recognised you, and that was forgotten every time Relay restarted (an update, a crash, a wake), so a restart while you were away left the Mac unguarded until you sat down again. Now, if you were recognised in the last 24 hours, it starts armed.
- **"Why didn't it alert?"** Relay now keeps a short record (the latest 30 stretches of time) of every time a face that was not clearly you was in view, and why it did or did not raise the alarm: it had not recognised you yet since starting; you had stopped an alarm with your fingerprint minutes before; the face matched you only weakly but above the limit (a look-alike such as a brother or sister); the face scored in the "not sure" zone; it was too small or turned away to judge; or it was seen too briefly. Ask "why didn't it alert my brother" or "what did the camera see while I was away" and it answers in words, with the scores.
- **"Make owner recognition stricter" / "looser".** Moves the match limit up or down by 0.05 (between 0.30 and 0.60; your own face usually scores above 0.7) and says what it did. Looser asks for your confirmation first.
- **The Jarvis voice stays a man's.** If the preferred Mac voice (Daniel) is missing, the fallback now tries other known British men, then other known men, before any voice that might be a woman's (it used to take the first British voice, whoever that was). The OmniVoice voice is already told to speak as a low-pitched British man.
- **A face setup window, guided like Glance.** "Enroll my face" (and the new "Set Up Owner Recognition…" item in the menu bar menu) now opens a window that shows what your camera sees, in a circle like a mirror, with a ring of eight directions around it. It walks you through nine head poses: straight ahead, then left, top left, up, top right, right, bottom right, down and bottom left. A pose is captured only when your head really is turned that way and has been held (half a second, three looks in a row, two samples per pose, 18 in all); the ring fills as you turn, a soft glow shows which way to go, and it tells you in words when you are too far, too close, turned too far, or when someone else is in the picture. If a pose is hard for your camera, the limits relax after 12 seconds so you are never stuck. Nothing is saved until all nine are done and the set covers straight ahead and at least six directions. Before, enrollment ran blind from a hidden camera page with no picture and no check that you had turned all around. Press D in the window to see the live head angles.
- **The standby screen can no longer trap you.** It said "the owner will be back soon" and stayed up until your face was recognised, so a camera that saw nobody (or stopped sending pictures) left you locked behind it. Now it does not appear while you have used the keyboard or mouse in the last 30 seconds (or your away time, if shorter), it clears after two seconds of touching the keyboard or mouse and stays down for two minutes, and it is taken down (and does not return) when the camera has stopped sending pictures. A stranger who sits down is still seen by the camera and gets the access-denied alarm; this only changes the calm standby screen.
- **The camera restarts itself after the Mac sleeps.** Closing the lid often leaves the camera stream dead or frozen, and nothing said so: Relay then saw nobody, which is what kept the standby screen up. The camera page now notices when its stream ends or its picture stops moving for 12 seconds and starts it again (after four restarts in two minutes it gives up and says so, so a really missing camera cannot loop), and Relay restarts the camera when the Mac wakes.
- **The alarm screen stays up while Touch ID is asked, and an accepted fingerprint always takes it down.** Before, the screen hid itself while the Touch ID sheet was up. Now it stays (only lowered one window level so the sheet shows above it, with the countdown still running and the siren still sounding, so pressing the button is not a way to silence the alarm, and the button reading "Touch the sensor…"), and goes back to the top if the fingerprint is not accepted. A fingerprint that is accepted now closes whichever screen is showing, including the standby test screen, which the guard did not own and which used to stay up until its timer ended.
- **Touch ID now comes up by itself when the access-denied alarm starts**, so touching the sensor stops it with no button to click first (it comes up once; if you cancel it the alarm comes back with its Touch ID button). The calm away screen still waits for your face.
- **Owner recognition answers faster when it is waiting for you.** The camera page now looks about 2.5 times a second (instead of once a second) only while Relay is waiting to see you: the first 30 seconds after turning recognition on, the away screen, and the access-denied countdown. Coming back from the away screen takes 0.8 seconds (two frames of you) instead of about 2. The rest of the time it is still once a second, and it never changes how many frames it takes to raise the alarm or to lock, so it is not more trigger-happy. The face model now loads as soon as recognition is on, not when the first face appears, so the first recognition after starting is not delayed.
- **Owner recognition can no longer be left without its camera.** If recognition was on and your face enrolled but Presence (the camera) had ended up off, nothing was being watched and Relay still read "on". Launching now starts the camera whenever recognition is on, tells you when it had to turn Presence back on, and a camera that fails to start for a passing reason is retried (15, 30 and 45 seconds later) instead of being switched off for good. Only a refused Camera permission, or running out of tries, leaves it off, and then it says so.
- **The startup check reports how long the window took to appear** (0.7 s after launch on a clean profile).

## 0.38.5

- **A startup check now guards every build.** `scripts/make-dmg.sh` launches the built app (a copy, with a throwaway profile, beside your running Relay) and refuses to make the .dmg unless it really starts: no startup errors in its log, the window's page has content, and the bridge to the app is there. If the app never answers it says a Keychain prompt is probably waiting. Run it yourself with `npm run startup-check`. Checked against the real broken 0.38.1: it fails it with "ReferenceError: fs is not defined".
- **`scripts/install-local.sh` (`npm run install-local`).** Installs the build into /Applications signed with your local "Relay Local Signing" certificate (the same identity every time, so macOS keeps the Keychain access, Accessibility, Camera and other permissions across updates), keeps the old app as a backup in ~/Downloads, tells you if this build would make macOS ask for the Keychain once (and to click Always Allow), and opens it.
- **Relay health in Doctor.** Doctor now also reports: Startup (how long it took, and any errors since, or that it never finished), the Keychain (can Relay read and write its saved keys), the last request that failed, the Voice, Owner recognition (enrolled, camera running, armed, best match score against the threshold), Ollama (and whether the search model is installed) and the `gh` tool. Ask "relay health", "show the health report" or "is Relay healthy".

## 0.38.3

- **Relay's window can no longer stay blank.** If the main window's page crashes, fails to load, stops responding, or comes up empty (for example after one of Chromium's helper processes died and restarted, or after the Mac woke up), Relay now loads it again by itself. It also checks the window each time it is shown again and a moment after the network helper restarts.
- **Honest failures (idea from Darwin).** When a model fails, Relay now says which brain and why, in plain words, instead of showing a raw error: "Claude is not installed on this Mac…", "Claude refused the request because the sign-in or key is not valid (it may have expired). Sign in again in Account", "Claude has used up its usage limit; it resets around 4:10 PM", "I could not reach Groq. Check the internet connection", "Ollama is not running. Open the Ollama app", "took too long", "empty reply", "request too long", "does not offer that model". A momentary rate limit (429) is told apart from a usage limit, Codex is named when it took over from Claude, and anything that looks like a key is hidden. When a better voice fails (ElevenLabs credit used up, a key refused, a missing permission), Relay now says so out loud in a notice and still answers in text or the system voice, once per problem every ten minutes.

## 0.38.2

- **Fixed: Relay's window opened blank and the app did not finish starting.** A line added in 0.38.0 (the plugin sandbox check) used `fs` without importing it, so startup stopped partway with "fs is not defined" and the window had nothing to show. Fixed, and a new test now reads every main-process file and fails if one uses `fs`, `path`, `os`, `crypto` and similar without declaring it.
- **Fixed: the Jarvis reactor and the "Welcome back" card stayed on screen.** The welcome-back card was built from the daily-briefing card, which is meant to stay until you close it, and the reactor waits for that card to close. The welcome card now closes by itself after 30 seconds (still longer while your mouse is over it, and never while pinned), and the reactor has a 60-second backstop so it can never be stuck.

## 0.38.1

- **Checked against OpenJarvis's own code and tests, and brought in line.** I read OpenJarvis's scanner, credential stripper, skill parser, Hacker News/RSS/GitHub/Obsidian connectors, BM25, rank fusion and deep-research agent, restated its test cases as `test/openjarvis-parity.test.js`, and fixed every difference they showed: the injection shield now also catches code-execution attempts, DAN-style jailbreaks, "pretend you have no restrictions", ```` ```system ```` delimiters, base64-then-send exfiltration and `; python …`-style command chains, and flagged results are wrapped as `<tool_result …>` like theirs; Hacker News reads the top five; feeds carry their text and dates and support a "since" cut-off; Obsidian notes use the header's title and tags (then the first heading, then the file name); imported skills must follow the standard's name rules (lowercase letters, digits, single hyphens, up to 64 characters), keep descriptions up to 1024 characters, and are flagged when they ask for shell, file-write or network-listening capabilities. Where Relay's version was weaker: RSS feeds could be pointed at your own network. Feeds are now fetched the way OpenJarvis does (`safeFetch.js`): public https only, the address is looked up and refused if any answer is private (including 169.254.169.254 and IPv6 local ranges), the connection is pinned to the checked address against DNS rebinding, and every redirect is checked again. Deep research now also searches your own notes first (a read-only `search_knowledge` tool), as OpenJarvis's agent does over its personal knowledge store.
