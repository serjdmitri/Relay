Owner recognition: faster, and it can no longer be left without its camera.

- **The camera could end up off while owner recognition still read "on".** Then nothing was watched and no alarm or away screen could work. Launching now starts the camera whenever recognition is on and your face is enrolled, tells you when it had to turn Presence back on, and retries a camera that fails to start for a passing reason (15, 30 and 45 seconds later) instead of switching it off for good. Only a refused Camera permission, or running out of tries, leaves it off, and then it says so. To stop the camera, turn owner recognition off.
- **A face setup window, guided like Glance.** "Enroll my face" (and a new "Set Up Owner Recognition…" item in the menu bar menu) opens a window showing what your camera sees, with a ring of eight directions around it. It walks you through nine head poses (straight ahead and the eight directions around it), two samples each, and captures a pose only when your head really is turned that way and held. The ring fills as you turn, a glow shows which way to go, and it says when you are too far, too close, turned too far, or not alone. Nothing is saved until the set covers straight ahead and at least six directions. Before, enrollment ran blind from a hidden camera with no picture. Press D in the window to see live head angles.
- **The standby screen can no longer trap you.** It said "the owner will be back soon" and stayed up until your face was recognised, so a camera that saw nobody (or stopped sending pictures) left you locked behind it. Now it does not appear while you have used the keyboard or mouse in the last 30 seconds (or your away time, if shorter), it clears after two seconds of touching the keyboard or mouse and stays down for two minutes, and it is taken down (and does not return) when the camera has stopped sending pictures. A stranger who sits down is still seen by the camera and gets the access-denied alarm; this only changes the calm standby screen.
- **The camera restarts itself after the Mac sleeps.** Closing the lid often leaves the camera stream dead or frozen, and nothing said so: Relay then saw nobody, which is what kept the standby screen up. The camera page now notices when its stream ends or its picture stops moving for 12 seconds and starts it again (after four restarts in two minutes it gives up and says so, so a really missing camera cannot loop), and Relay restarts the camera when the Mac wakes.
- **The alarm screen stays up while Touch ID is asked, and an accepted fingerprint always takes it down.** Before, the screen hid itself while the Touch ID sheet was up. Now it stays (only lowered one window level so the sheet shows above it, with the countdown still running and the siren quiet, and the button reading "Touch the sensor…"), and goes back to the top if the fingerprint is not accepted. A fingerprint that is accepted now closes whichever screen is showing, including the standby test screen, which the guard did not own and which used to stay up until its timer ended.
- **Touch ID comes up by itself when the access-denied alarm starts**, so touching the sensor stops it with no button to click first (once; cancel it and the alarm comes back with its button).
- **Faster recognition when it is waiting for you.** The camera looks about 2.5 times a second (instead of once) only while Relay is waiting to see you: the first 30 seconds after turning recognition on, the away screen, and the access-denied countdown. Coming back from the away screen takes 0.8 seconds instead of about 2. It never changes how many frames it takes to raise the alarm or lock, so it is not more trigger-happy. The face model now loads when recognition turns on, not when the first face appears.
- **The startup check reports how long the window took to appear** (0.7 s after launch on a clean profile).
- **Includes Codex's 0.38.6 to 0.38.8** (never published on their own): the God's Eye globe floats over the desktop with optional hover side controls; natural camera, place and layer commands go straight to the map; map readiness follows the engine instead of a fixed wait, and a closed world is kept hidden for two minutes for a quick reopen; short local greetings are prepared ahead as private cached audio (replies and conversations are never cached); local speech no longer decrypts unrelated cloud keys on every request. Codex measured God's Eye command-ready at 3.1 to 4.2 s (was 6.2 s) and a prepared greeting dispatching in about 1 ms. See `docs/release-notes-v0.38.6.md` and `docs/release-notes-v0.38.8.md`.

The full test suite passes (1343 tests).
