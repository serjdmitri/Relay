Owner recognition: faster, and it can no longer be left without its camera.

- **The camera could end up off while owner recognition still read "on".** Then nothing was watched and no alarm or away screen could work. Launching now starts the camera whenever recognition is on and your face is enrolled, tells you when it had to turn Presence back on, and retries a camera that fails to start for a passing reason (15, 30 and 45 seconds later) instead of switching it off for good. Only a refused Camera permission, or running out of tries, leaves it off, and then it says so. To stop the camera, turn owner recognition off.
- **"Enroll my face" no longer leaves the reactor in front of the setup window.** Its reply is now short and tells Relay not to hold it on screen (and not to ask "anything else?"), so the reactor goes after a moment, and the setup window sits above the reactor and the answer card. Only this command changed; every other answer stays until dismissed as before.
- **Trusted people are now recognised as themselves.** Before, a trusted person's face was mixed into the list that means "you", so Relay took them for you, and the second-viewer guard counted them as a stranger and started its lock countdown. Now the camera page compares each face with you and, separately, with your trusted profiles. A trusted person (who has also blinked, so a photo of them does not count) is neither you nor a stranger: they never start the access-denied alarm, they are not counted as an extra viewer, they cannot arm the guard or lift the standby screen as if they were you, and an alarm that a stranger started ends once only trusted people are left in view. A stranger beside a trusted person still starts the alarm and the countdown. Tested with a real camera (your face as "trusted": trusted score 0.87, owner score -0.09, flagged trusted in 31 of 31 looks; as "owner": owner 0.85, trusted -0.11, flagged trusted 0 of 23).
- **"Use Twilio for calls" / "switch to the free calling model".** A voice command that sets only which provider places calls (Twilio, Bland, or automatic). It never takes or changes a key or token: for Twilio it tells you that the account SID, auth token and Twilio phone number are yours to add in Calls settings, and that automatic check-ins stay paused until recreated, because they were authorized for the old provider. The reply after "call me about..." now names the provider actually used instead of always saying Bland. Twilio reads the message in the British male voice Brian (Polly.Brian); a scheduled, authorized call through Twilio is covered by a new test.
- **"Take me to…" on the phone now shows a route preview inside Relay (finished from Codex's work).** On the phone page, "take me to NSU University School", "walk me to Starbucks" or "directions to Miami Airport" opens a Jarvis Navigation page with an interactive map (MapLibre, OpenStreetMap data from OpenFreeMap), place search (Photon) and a walking or driving route with distance, time and step-by-step directions (Valhalla). It replaces the "Preview in Jarvis Navigation" link that needed a separate iPhone app that was never installed; "Open Apple Maps for live navigation" stays as the way to navigate for real. It started as a preview only (live turn-by-turn guidance is the next item). Location is optional and read once, only when you tap its button (or search for a starting place instead). These are free public services meant for light use and can be unavailable, in which case the page says so and offers Apple Maps. The requests carry no pairing token and no AI model sees them, they are blocked in local/private mode, and the page works only for a paired phone. Settings > Phone shows a "Phone map providers" card to switch to your own compatible servers (HTTPS or localhost only, no credentials in the URLs). MapLibre adds about 29 MB to the Mac app; the phone loads only its browser files. Found and fixed while testing against the live server: Valhalla returns its route line in an encoded form even when asked for plain coordinates, so Relay now decodes it (bounded, with malformed shapes rejected). Tested: place search, routes, caching, rate limits, private mode and the paired-phone checks, a live route request, and the page itself at a phone-sized window (map, route, directions, and a cancelled route not coming back late).
- **Jarvis Navigation on the phone: turn-by-turn guidance, like Apple Maps, with the map floating at the top.** The "take me to…" page is now a real navigation screen. The map sits at the top with its edges fading into the dark (the God's Eye look) and Jarvis's colours; the trip is in a panel below it (arrival time, time left, distance to go). Say or type "take me to Starbucks", "walk to the beach", then "start": the page follows your position, tilts and turns the map with you, shows the next turn at the top, and Jarvis speaks it as you approach ("In half a mile, turn left onto Beta Street", then "In 800 feet…", then "Turn left."; "You have arrived at Starbucks, Sir."). Wrong turn: "Recalculating, Sir." and a new route. The microphone button takes spoken commands (start, stop, mute, repeat, what's next, how far, zoom, overview, recenter, Apple Maps). The voice is the phone's own British male voice (Daniel on an iPhone), not the Mac's. Honest limits: a web page on an iPhone stops guiding when it is in the background or the phone locks (the screen is kept awake while navigating, and Apple Maps is one tap away), live position needs the secure connection the phone already uses, and there is no always-on "Hey Jarvis" on the phone: tap the microphone. Tested with a simulated drive through the real page (spoken lines, banner, wrong-turn reroute, arrival) and 15 new tests.
- **A friend who kept turning or leaning at the Mac no longer slips past the alarm.** The alarm needs five clear looks at a stranger in a row, and any look that could not be judged (face too small, turned away, in the grey zone) reset that count to zero. A real case from your Mac: 24 seconds with 9 stranger looks, 12 unjudgeable and 1 unsure, interleaved, so the count never reached five and nothing happened. Looks that cannot be judged now pause the count instead of erasing it. Your own face, an empty chair, or more than 12 unclear looks in a row still reset it, so you looking away does not start it. "Why didn't it alert" gave the right reason ("too small, too far or turned away"); this fixes the cause.
- **"Hey Jarvis" on the phone.** The phone page now has a switch in its menu (and an ear button on the navigation map) that listens for "Hey Jarvis" with the phone's own speech recognition. Say "Hey Jarvis, lock my Mac" and it is sent like a typed command, or say "Hey Jarvis" alone and then the command; on the map, "Hey Jarvis, what's next" works while you drive. It accepts how the recogniser tends to mishear it ("hey Jarvus", "ok Harvis", a bare "Jarvis" at the start). It pauses while the phone is speaking or you hold the reactor, so it does not hear itself, and it stops with a clear message if the microphone is refused or the speech service keeps failing. Honest limits: a web page can only listen while it is open on screen with the phone unlocked (the page keeps the screen awake while it is on, which uses battery), it is off until you turn it on, and Safari wants one tap after the page opens before the microphone starts. Not like the Mac, which listens all the time. Tested with 7 new tests and a probe of the real pages with a fake recogniser (it also found and fixed a browser-only bug in the timer handling).
- **"Hey Jarvis" and the Option key can no longer both go dead together.** While you record a new shortcut, Relay pauses its shortcuts and "Hey Jarvis" so the key you press is not also obeyed. That pause was only ever turned off by the settings window, so closing, hiding or reloading it mid-recording left both paused until Relay restarted, with the screen still saying Ready. The pause now ends by itself after 15 seconds and whenever the window is hidden, loses focus or reloads. (Found by reading the code after both stopped at once; I could not confirm on your Mac that this is what happened, since Relay writes no log.)
- **Relay learns the people you name and starts naming them itself.** When you name a visitor (in the pictures window, or by saying "that's Sam"), Relay keeps their face as a profile (encrypted, up to 12 faces, never a picture). After you have named someone a couple of times, a new visitor who looks like them gets a question instead of an assumption: "Is that Sam? Say yes or no", asked when you come back, spoken if replies are spoken, and with Yes / No buttons in the pictures window. Each yes adds the face; a no records that face as not them (it is never offered as them again) and sets the learning back; "no, that's Alex" names the visitor in the same breath. After three yeses in a row the person is learned and later visits are named without asking ("Sam (I recognised the face from earlier visits)"). A face must match well and clearly better than anyone else you have named to be offered; a face that looks like nobody is never named. A plain "yes" or "no" is only heard for five minutes after Relay asked, so it is never taken for anything else. "Who do you know?" lists the people; "forget Sam's face" (or the Forget button, after Touch ID) removes a profile and its name from their visits. Before, a visit whose face matched a named one simply copied the name; now a name only comes from you or from a person Relay has learned and you have confirmed. Checked with the faces from a real scan: after two namings it asked for the next three angles (scores 0.73 to 0.86), learned the person after three yeses, named the rest by itself, and never suggested an unrelated face.
- **Pictures of strangers, and "who was at your Mac".** When the access-denied alarm starts for someone who is not you (never the test screen), Relay takes up to three pictures of the camera view, a few seconds apart, and keeps them encrypted on this Mac (the same protection as your face data, as separate files; the newest 30 visits for up to 60 days). It also keeps the stranger's face numbers, so a second visit by the same face is recognised ("someone I have seen before", with the date of the earlier visit). When you come back (the first time the camera sees you after the alarm, or when you unlock the Mac) Relay tells you: who was there as far as it can know (it cannot know a name, so it says "someone I do not recognise", or "someone I have seen before"), when, how many pictures and how it ended (you came back, your fingerprint, a trusted person, or the screen locked), and opens a window with the pictures. In that window you can give a visit a name, and later visits by the same face carry it; deleting a visit asks you to confirm with Touch ID first, so a stranger cannot erase the evidence. Say "show the intruder pictures" or "who was at my computer" to see them any time, "turn off intruder photos" to stop taking them, and "delete the intruder photos" to remove them all (that also asks for confirmation). Tested with a real camera (snapshots, stored encrypted, the same face recognised on a second visit).
- **After saving your face, Relay shows how accurate and reliable the scan was, and offers to redo weak angles.** Once the nine poses are saved it asks you to look at the camera for a moment (a live check with fresh looks that were not part of the scan), then shows three numbers and a chip for each angle. Accuracy is the share of scanned angles that are recognised from the other angles; reliability is how far above the match threshold they score on average (0% at the threshold, 100% at 0.9, set from a real scan of the owner); the live check is how many of the fresh looks were recognised. A scan is "reliable" at 90% accuracy and 80% reliability with no weak angle. Below that you are told which angles were weakest and offered "Redo the weak angles" (only those are captured again, and the straight-ahead position is kept) or "Redo everything"; below 75% it says the scan is not reliable. It is an estimate from one sitting, so a different room or light can score lower.
- **The build's startup check no longer calls a build broken when the Mac is locked.** Relay reads the Keychain when it starts, which cannot be done while the screen is locked, so a check run then hung and looked like a failure. It now notices the locked screen, waits up to 15 minutes for it to be unlocked, and if it stays locked says so and stops (instead of reporting a broken build). It also retries once on a plain failure, and no longer mistakes a leftover macOS SecurityAgent process for an open Keychain prompt.
- **A fingerprint stop means it was you.** When the alarm is stopped with your fingerprint, the pictures taken for it are deleted and never shown: that was you, not an intruder.
- **It keeps guarding while you are away, even after Relay restarts.** The guard only begins to guard once it has recognised you, and that was forgotten every time Relay restarted (an update, a crash, a wake), so a restart while you were away left the Mac unguarded until you sat down again. Now, if you were recognised in the last 24 hours, it starts armed.
- **"Why didn't it alert?"** Relay now keeps a short record (the latest 30 stretches of time) of every time a face that was not clearly you was in view, and why it did or did not raise the alarm: it had not recognised you yet since starting; you had stopped an alarm with your fingerprint minutes before; the face matched you only weakly but above the limit (a look-alike such as a brother or sister); the face scored in the "not sure" zone; it was too small or turned away to judge; or it was seen too briefly. Ask "why didn't it alert my brother" or "what did the camera see while I was away" and it answers in words, with the scores.
- **"Make owner recognition stricter" / "looser".** Moves the match limit up or down by 0.05 (between 0.30 and 0.60; your own face usually scores above 0.7) and says what it did. Looser asks for your confirmation first.
- **The Jarvis voice stays a man's.** If the preferred Mac voice (Daniel) is missing, the fallback now tries other known British men, then other known men, before any voice that might be a woman's (it used to take the first British voice, whoever that was). The OmniVoice voice is already told to speak as a low-pitched British man.
- **A face setup window, guided like Glance.** "Enroll my face" (and a new "Set Up Owner Recognition…" item in the menu bar menu) opens a window showing what your camera sees, with a ring of eight directions around it. It walks you through nine head poses (straight ahead and the eight directions around it), two samples each, and captures a pose only when your head really is turned that way and held. The ring fills as you turn, a glow shows which way to go, and it says when you are too far, too close, turned too far, or not alone. Nothing is saved until the set covers straight ahead and at least six directions. Before, enrollment ran blind from a hidden camera with no picture. Press D in the window to see live head angles.
- **The standby screen can no longer trap you.** It said "the owner will be back soon" and stayed up until your face was recognised, so a camera that saw nobody (or stopped sending pictures) left you locked behind it. Now it does not appear while you have used the keyboard or mouse in the last 30 seconds (or your away time, if shorter), it clears after two seconds of touching the keyboard or mouse and stays down for two minutes, and it is taken down (and does not return) when the camera has stopped sending pictures. A stranger who sits down is still seen by the camera and gets the access-denied alarm; this only changes the calm standby screen.
- **The camera restarts itself after the Mac sleeps.** Closing the lid often leaves the camera stream dead or frozen, and nothing said so: Relay then saw nobody, which is what kept the standby screen up. The camera page now notices when its stream ends or its picture stops moving for 12 seconds and starts it again (after four restarts in two minutes it gives up and says so, so a really missing camera cannot loop), and Relay restarts the camera when the Mac wakes.
- **The alarm screen stays up while Touch ID is asked, and an accepted fingerprint always takes it down.** Before, the screen hid itself while the Touch ID sheet was up. Now it stays (only lowered one window level so the sheet shows above it, with the countdown still running and the siren still sounding, so pressing the button is not a way to silence the alarm, and the button reading "Touch the sensor…"), and goes back to the top if the fingerprint is not accepted. A fingerprint that is accepted now closes whichever screen is showing, including the standby test screen, which the guard did not own and which used to stay up until its timer ended.
- **Touch ID comes up by itself when the access-denied alarm starts**, so touching the sensor stops it with no button to click first (once; cancel it and the alarm comes back with its button).
- **Faster recognition when it is waiting for you.** The camera looks about 2.5 times a second (instead of once) only while Relay is waiting to see you: the first 30 seconds after turning recognition on, the away screen, and the access-denied countdown. Coming back from the away screen takes 0.8 seconds instead of about 2. It never changes how many frames it takes to raise the alarm or lock, so it is not more trigger-happy. The face model now loads when recognition turns on, not when the first face appears.
- **The startup check reports how long the window took to appear** (0.7 s after launch on a clean profile).
- **Includes Codex's 0.38.6 to 0.38.8** (never published on their own): the God's Eye globe floats over the desktop with optional hover side controls; natural camera, place and layer commands go straight to the map; map readiness follows the engine instead of a fixed wait, and a closed world is kept hidden for two minutes for a quick reopen; short local greetings are prepared ahead as private cached audio (replies and conversations are never cached); local speech no longer decrypts unrelated cloud keys on every request. Codex measured God's Eye command-ready at 3.1 to 4.2 s (was 6.2 s) and a prepared greeting dispatching in about 1 ms. See `docs/release-notes-v0.38.6.md` and `docs/release-notes-v0.38.8.md`. It also includes Codex's 0.38.10 to 0.38.12 (reliability and phone navigation, the wake handoff fix, reactor recovery): see `docs/release-notes-v0.38.10.md`, `v0.38.11.md` and `v0.38.12.md`.
